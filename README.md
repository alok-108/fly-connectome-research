# Fly Connectome Research

A computational neurodynamics framework investigating how anatomical wiring topology, recurrent coupling scales, external stimulation, and targeted structural lesions govern population activity and vulnerability in the *Drosophila melanogaster* (fruit fly) connectome using empirical electron-microscopy wiring diagrams and lightweight spiking point-neuron models.

---

## Research Question

How do connectome topology, recurrent synaptic coupling strength, external stimulation volume, and targeted structural perturbations interact to determine dynamical activity, recruitment, and vulnerability in a connectome-constrained Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) network?

Specifically, the project investigates whether high-degree "hub" neurons provide disproportionate support to recurrent dynamics compared to random neuron populations, and how this vulnerability is modulated by synaptic coupling regimes and external driving inputs.

## Dataset

The project uses the **Janelia FlyEM Drosophila Hemibrain Connectome (v1.2.1)**:
- **Biological Scope**: Central brain of an adult female *Drosophila melanogaster*, encompassing the central complex (CX), mushroom bodies (MB), antennal lobes (AL), and lateral horn (LH).
- **Scale**: 21,739 proofread neurons, 3,550,403 unique directed neuron-neuron connections, and 14,329,229 reconstructed synaptic contacts (presynaptic T-bars and postsynaptic densities [PSDs]).
- **Provenance**: Generated by the Janelia Research Campus (HHMI) and Google Research (Scheffer et al., 2020). Curated network archive mirrored via Netzschleuder (Tiago Peixoto).
- **Primary Processed Subnetwork**:
  - Size: **1,000 neurons**
  - Selection Strategy: `highest_degree` ($k_{\text{total}} = k_{\text{in}} + k_{\text{out}}$, seed 42)
  - Directed Edges: **113,089**
  - Total Synaptic Weight: **937,685** anatomical synapse contacts
  - Graph Density: $\rho \approx 0.1132$
  - Average Total Degree: $\langle k \rangle \approx 226.18$
  - Maximum Total Degree: $k_{\max} = 924$
  - Connected Components: Single weakly connected component ($100\%$ of surviving neurons).
- **Format**: Converted via chunked PyArrow streaming into standardized, memory-efficient Apache Parquet files (`data/processed/neurons.parquet` and `connections.parquet`).

## Computational Model

The network dynamics are simulated using a **connectome-constrained Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF)** architecture:
- **Point LIF Dynamics**: Subthreshold membrane potential integrates passive leak, connectome-derived synaptic currents, injected external drive, and background Gaussian noise:
  $$\tau_m \frac{dV_i(t)}{dt} = -(V_i(t) - V_{\text{rest}}) + R_m I_{\text{ext}, i}(t) + I_{\text{syn}, i}(t) + \xi_i(t)$$
  with fixed biophysical constants: $\tau_m = 20\,\text{ms}$, $V_{\text{rest}} = -65\,\text{mV}$, $V_{\text{reset}} = -70\,\text{mV}$, $V_{\text{threshold}} = -50\,\text{mV}$, and refractory period $t_{\text{ref}} = 2\,\text{ms}$.
- **Directed Weighted Synaptic Coupling**: When presynaptic neuron $j$ fires, postsynaptic neuron $i$ receives an instantaneous potential jump:
  $$\Delta V_i = \alpha \cdot W_{ji}$$
  where $W_{ji}$ is the reconstructed anatomical synapse count and $\alpha$ is the synaptic coupling scale ($\text{mV / synapse contact}$).
- **External Stimulation Protocol**: Square-wave current pulses ($I_{\text{ext}} = 18.0\,\text{mV}$, duration $400\,\text{ms}$, active from $t = 200\,\text{ms}$ to $600\,\text{ms}$) injected into a predefined fraction of the network.
- **Lesion Model**: Neuronal ablation removes nodes and all incident edges prior to simulation, evaluating network degradation on copies without modifying the underlying dataset.

### Essential Model Simplifications & Boundary Conditions
1. **Excitatory Synaptic Representation**: All anatomical contacts are represented as positive membrane potential jumps; inhibitory/excitatory polarity is not differentiated.
2. **Computational Coupling Scale**: $\alpha$ is a computational model parameter, **not an in vivo biophysically calibrated quantal conductance**.
3. **No Neuromodulation or Gap Junctions**: Volume transmission and electrical synapses are omitted.
4. **Subnetwork Bias**: The 1,000-neuron highest-degree subnetwork is an anatomically dense core and is not representative of the entire fly brain.
5. **No Behavioral Equivalence**: Activity changes reflect computational dynamics of an integrate-and-fire model, not organismal behavioral phenotypes.

## Key Findings (Phases 1–7)

- **Coupling-Dependent Regimes (Phase 6)**:
  - At weak coupling ($\alpha \le 0.010\,\text{mV/synapse}$), activity is dominated by direct external drive, unstimulated neurons remain largely subthreshold, and post-stimulus activity is absent ($0.00\,\text{Hz}$).
  - In the transition region ($\alpha \approx 0.015–0.030\,\text{mV/synapse}$), recurrent synaptic input recruits unstimulated neurons and sustains reverberant activity after stimulus offset.
  - At high coupling ($\alpha \ge 0.040\,\text{mV/synapse}$), the excitatory-only model exhibits numerical instability due to runaway positive feedback.
- **Regime-Dependent Hub Vulnerability (Phase 6 & 7)**:
  - At $\alpha = 0.010$, mean population firing rates appear buffered under lesions primarily due to proportional normalization arithmetic ($N_{\text{surviving}}$).
  - In contrast, in the recurrent regime ($\alpha = 0.030$), targeted hub lesions cause substantial functional collapse compared to random deletions (e.g. 10% hub lesion reduces activity robustness $R_{\text{act}}$ to $0.1935$, while 20% random lesion retains $R_{\text{act}} = 0.4030 \pm 0.0881$).
- **Local Continuity Around Transition (Phase 7A)**:
  - The measured recruitment and firing-rate metrics increased monotonically across the tested $\alpha$ grid; $\alpha = 0.0200$ is a representative point within a broader transition region rather than an isolated mathematical threshold.
- **Stimulus-Drive Interaction (Phase 7B)**:
  - When external stimulation engages recurrent recruitment ($\ge 10\%$), targeted hub lesions consistently cause greater reductions in simulated activity than random removals.
- **Timestep Sensitivity (Phase 7D)**:
  - The qualitative ordering of hub versus random lesion effects was preserved across the tested timesteps ($0.25\,\text{ms}, 0.50\,\text{ms}, 1.00\,\text{ms}$), although absolute firing rates and robustness values showed appreciable timestep dependence.
- **Subnetwork Topology Contrast (Phase 7E)**:
  - Within the tested subnetworks, strong recurrent recruitment and hub-lesion sensitivity were observed in the degree-enriched central subnetwork but not in the randomly sampled subnetwork at the same coupling values.

## Reproducibility Guide

### 1. Environment Setup

```powershell
# Clone or navigate to the repository
cd fly-connectome-research

# Create a virtual environment
python -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1

# Install dependencies
python -m pip install --upgrade pip
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
```

### 2. Verify Repository Tests
Confirm all 64 unit tests pass:
```powershell
pytest -v
```

### 3. Verify Result Integrity
Run the Phase 7 reproducibility validation:
```powershell
python scratch/verify_phase7_reproducibility.py
```

### 4. Running Experiments
All experiments use deterministic pseudo-random seeds:
```powershell
# Experiment 01: Connectome subnetwork extraction & diagnostics
python experiments/01_connectome_exploration.py --neurons 1000 --strategy highest_degree --seed 42

# Experiment 02: Baseline LIF simulation
python experiments/02_baseline_simulation.py --neurons 1000 --duration 1000 --dt 0.5 --weight-scale 0.01 --stimulus pulse --seed 42

# Experiment 03: Virtual lesion experiments (Phase 4)
python experiments/03_lesion_experiments.py --neurons 1000 --seed 42

# Experiment 04: Stimulus robustness experiments (Phase 5)
python experiments/04_stimulus_robustness.py --seed 42

# Experiment 05: Coupling-regime sweep (Phase 6)
python experiments/05_coupling_sweep.py --seed 42

# Experiment 06: Sensitivity and robustness validation (Phase 7)
python experiments/06_phase7_robustness.py
```

## Limitations

1. **Absence of Synaptic Inhibition**: In the current point-LIF model, all connectome edges contribute positive depolarizing currents. Because opposing inhibitory populations are omitted, high coupling regimes lead to runaway excitation.
2. **Coupling Parameter Interpretation**: The synaptic coupling scalar $\alpha$ represents an instantaneous potential jump ($\text{mV / synapse contact}$) rather than a biophysically grounded conductance with reversal potentials and kinetic filtering.
3. **Subnetwork Selection Bias**: The 1,000-neuron highest-degree subnetwork enriches for densely connected central brain integration hubs. Conclusions cannot be directly generalized to sparser, feedforward brain regions or the whole brain.
4. **Structural vs. Functional Connectivity**: Connectome graphs measure reconstructed anatomical synapse contacts. Physiological functional connectivity depends on receptor subtypes, modulatory states, and internal neural dynamics not captured in static EM graphs.
5. **No Behavioral Equivalence**: Simulated firing rate changes evaluate network-level signal propagation under idealized point-neuron assumptions and must not be interpreted as claims about live animal behavior or cognitive performance.
